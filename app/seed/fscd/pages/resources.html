Resources + Training

Knowledge That Stays in the Community.

FSCD treats training and local capacity as essential infrastructure. Across rural and underserved communities globally, with initial program development focused on India, the goal is not simply to introduce technology or fund a project. It is to help local people understand systems, operate them, maintain them, teach others and build on them.

How Learning Becomes Capacity

Not a Workshop.
A Capability Cycle.

Training is most valuable when it changes what people can do after the trainer leaves. FSCD’s learning model can be organized around a repeatable cycle: understand, practice, apply, document, teach and improve.

01
Understand the SystemLearn the purpose, workflow, roles, risks and community outcome the program is designed to support.
02
Practice the WorkUse demonstrations, guided practice and real operating scenarios instead of passive instruction alone.
03
Apply LocallyAdapt the process to the facility, language, workforce, care environment and community context.
04
Document & MaintainMake day-to-day care, records, troubleshooting and ownership part of the operating routine.
05
Teach the Next PersonBuild local trainers and institutional memory so knowledge does not disappear with staff turnover.
06
Improve the SystemFeed field experience back into training, implementation design and future program decisions.

Regional + Global Knowledge Network

Share What Works. Keep It Local.

FSCD’s broader mission is community-driven development in rural and underserved regions. With initial program development focused on India, the knowledge network is designed to let partners share experience across communities globally without flattening local differences or replacing local leadership.

Learning LoopsField experience improves future training and implementation.

Local AdaptationResources can be translated and adjusted for language, context and facility needs.

Peer LearningLocal teams can learn from other implementers facing similar challenges.

Institutional MemoryTraining becomes part of the organization and community—not one person’s knowledge.
