Clean Energy Access Program and Planned Expansion

Build on a Proven Model. Expand Responsibly.

FSCD plans to collaborate with strategic partners — the International Institute for Energy Conservation (IIEC), Aptiv India, Syneos Health India, and the Limaye Foundation — to expand the Clean Energy Access Program. Future deployments remain subject to required agreements, government approvals, funding, site readiness, and implementation arrangements.

The Challenge

Reliable Power Is Essential Rural Infrastructure.

In rural India, Primary Health Centers (PHCs) and schools are critical to essential health and education services, yet unreliable electricity can sharply limit what those institutions are able to provide. PHCs may struggle with vaccine storage, medical equipment and emergency readiness, while schools can be constrained in their use of ICT tools and digital learning. Closing this infrastructure gap with clean, uninterrupted energy can strengthen the resilience and quality of rural public services and reduce dependence on distant urban centers for basic needs.

The IIEC / FSCD Model

Clean Energy That Strengthens Health & Education.

Since 2017, IIEC in India has implemented its Clean Energy Access Program to strengthen health and education services in remote villages. The program began in Uttarakhand and is now active in Haryana, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, and Karnataka. It provides 24x7 electricity to PHCs and schools so facilities can support vaccine storage, medical equipment, lighting, ICT tools and digital education. FSCD plans to build on this operating model through future collaboration and expansion.

IIEC’s model connects solar infrastructure with community engagement, government coordination, local training and long-term maintenance — so dependable power supports dependable public services.

From Installation to Ownership

A Deployment Model Built to Stay Useful.

The work does not stop when panels are installed. FSCD’s delivery model is designed to combine equipment, local coordination, training, documentation, maintenance planning, and a clear transition of responsibility to the institutions being served.

Explore Partnerships
Resilient InfrastructurePower that belongs to the community.

Solar generation, battery backup, efficient equipment, training, and institutional ownership are designed to work as one system.

01

Assess the Facility

Identify energy needs around the services that matter most: medical equipment, refrigeration, lighting, ICT, and continuous operations.

02

Install the Energy System

Deploy solar systems with battery backup and inverters, plus supporting energy-efficient equipment where required.

03

Coordinate Locally

Coordinate with participating schools, clinics or PHCs, relevant government entities, local leadership, and community stakeholders during implementation.

04

Train for Daily Care

Provide staff training, local-language guidance, documentation, and day-to-day maintenance procedures.

05

Transfer Ownership

Move system ownership to the school or PHC with commitments around maintenance, aftercare, and safety.

06

Build the Next Layer

Use reliable energy as a platform for stronger education, health care access, digital services, and future community programs.

Planned Program Expansion

From 110 Sites Toward 200–250.

FSCD plans to expand the current 110-site Clean Energy Access footprint to approximately 200–250 sites over the next several years. At selected PHCs, FSCD also plans to enhance services with free vision care using Eyer2 devices, together with low-cost eyeglasses and frames. Expansion remains subject to required agreements, government approvals, funding, site readiness and implementation arrangements.
